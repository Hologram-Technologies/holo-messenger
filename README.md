# Holo Messenger

A serverless messenger client with the feature set of Element, built on uor-comms and Prism ID.

## Status

Step 1 (reading, mapping, inventory) and the client-only Phase 1 rules are done. The interface in `client/` is for real people only (2026-11-21): it always runs on the uor-comms engine, with real messages between real browsers. Nothing in it is made up: the sample mode, its people, chats, scripted replies and simulated situations were removed (see Engine).

This repository is an instance of `UOR-Foundation/template`. Its Prism application, `Messenger rules`, answers eleven client questions with the rules proved here.

For the engine's state, read uor-comms `docs/STATUS.md`; this repository does not restate it.

## Upstream

uor-comms is the canonical reference; Holo Messenger is a downstream client of it (Ilya, 2026-11-19). Where this repository and uor-comms disagree on the engine's design, phases, behaviour or status, uor-comms is right and this repository is corrected. Read these there, not here:

- `docs/DESIGN.md`: the design and the decisions taken.
- `docs/STATUS.md`: what Lean proved, what is tested, what is not done.
- `docs/MATRIX-MAP.md`: what Matrix specifies and where each server job goes.
- `host/README.md`: the host's calls and what the caller supplies.

This repository owns only the client: the interface in `client/`, the client rules in `app/`, and the inventory of what a person sees and does. Its documents about uor-comms (`docs/inventory/matrix-to-uor-comms-map.md`, `docs/reading/uor-comms-report.md`, and the inventory's "uor-comms need" and "engine standing" columns) are dated snapshots kept for the record. The engine's own phase plan, when uor-comms publishes one, decides when each engine-backed item can be built.

## Engine

The interface runs only on the uor-comms engine, with real messages between real browsers (Ilya, 2026-11-21: "remove all fake people and content", "i want to test it with real users"). There is no sample mode and no switch: a first visit shows the welcome, makes the identity and device key, asks your name, shows one notice of what is real, and opens an empty chat list with two actions, Invite someone (a `#uc1.` link and its picture code) and Open an invite. A reload resumes on the chats kept in the browser.

The first-run notice, through `explain(why, how, what)` on one screen: "Why: Holo Messenger sends real messages between real browsers, and no server keeps them. It is early: nothing is encrypted yet, so send nothing sensitive. How: Connection setup goes through ntfy.sh, a public server; the people you connect to and a public STUN server (Google's) see your network address. Both people must be online at the same time. What: Test with someone on the same network: until the relay exists, two people on different networks may not connect. Invite someone with your link, or open an invite someone sent you."

Names: the engine has no names and there are no profiles yet, so each person names their contacts on their own device (Ilya's choice). Your own name is kept on this device and others do not see it; the page says so. When you open someone's link, join a direct chat from someone you have not named, or someone opens your link, the page asks what to call them. The name is kept only in this browser (the page's database, with the keys), editable from their card as "Name only you see", and never sent. Everywhere a person appears (list, chat header, bubbles, members, mentions, reactions, system lines) the page shows that name, else the start of their identity; unnamed group members show "Add name". A mention is sent by identity, so each person reads it under the name they gave.

What works between real people: direct chats and groups; invite by link both ways; invite, join, decline, leave, remove, ban, lift a ban and roles, each offered only where `offered()` says; send, reply, edit, react (one reaction per person, the page taking back the earlier one through the engine), delete for everyone, forward (text), mentions, search in a chat and across chats, paging, reload restore, the outbox and Try again, removal shown read-only to the removed, refusals in plain words, typing notices (#33), and a placeholder for content of a newer version or kind. Kept on this page only, as preferences of the browser: favourites, pinned chats, notification levels and mute, drafts, what you have read (so unread counts survive a reload), recent searches.

Not built, so not shown, and why: pictures, files, the camera and voice (media waits on encryption; message content version 1 holds text only); read receipts, message info's readers and "online" (the engine cannot know); verifying other people and your own devices; the recovery kit and linked devices (the device key is made non-extractable, so this identity lives in this browser only, and the page says so); blocking and reporting (no engine support); message pins; "Anyone can join" (until a person not invited can obtain a room, uor-comms item 8, a group is invite only). Room names, descriptions and pictures are wired, but until uor-comms delivers settings bytes to other members (confirmed bug, fix pending) the other members see the group as "Group · N people" and its info says the name has not reached this browser; no name is made up. Testing across different networks waits on uor-comms's member relay (no third-party TURN).

Pinned: version `4230a1379889507a9e1f79b1d63497e9afc6061d30ed49add425039cb61a1075`, published from uor-comms commit `bed745bc482b80271f8404e126b4701262bf1f3d` (#33), checked file by file and against the version on 2026-11-21. What it adds to `922c5261`: `transport.typing(room, on)`, `transport.typingIn(room)` and `transport.onTyping` (a notice lasts 6 seconds unless renewed, is accepted only from a proven peer that may see the room, and is never stored); the page renews its own every 3 seconds while you type, turns it off when you send, empty the box or leave the chat, sends none while Privacy, Send typing notices is off, and draws others' through `Layout.headerLine` with the names you gave. And `messages()` lists content of a newer version or unknown kind as `{ unknown: true, version, kind }`; the page draws it as the placeholder (`Timeline.unsupportedShown`), and `send` refuses to write such content (`content`). The room-settings delivery fix is not in this version. Before that, `922c5261` (82dae7a): room settings are events (action 10). `engine.state(room)` answers the name, description and picture (content addresses, or null), whether the room is open, the power each of invite, kick, ban, unban, send, setPower and redact needs, the power to change settings, and whether the room is direct; `changeSetting(room, setting, value)` changes one of them and `mayChangeSetting(room, setting, value)` answers whether that change would be accepted. The kernel decides (`Comms.Settings::changeComposes`): a joined member with the settings power, never to a power above their own, and in a direct room only the name, description and picture. `offered()`, admission, redaction and refusals now read each action's own required power from the room, so the defaults changed: inviting and sending need 0; removing, banning, lifting a ban, changing roles, deleting another member's message and changing settings need 50; a room is invited only until someone opens it. A power-0 member who tries to remove someone now hears `needs power`, not `not allowed`. And #32: a peer whose setup stalls is dropped after `setupWithin` (15 seconds) and set up again at the next hello, with no change to the API. Before that, `36a21d8a` (#30, carrying #22 to #29) brought content read in place (4,096 bytes of text), the outbox and `retry`, a room address per group, introductions, invitations with the room so far, `timeline(room, { before, limit })`, `messages()` with edits and reactions applied, `redact`, redacted content deleted from each store, `mine` and `mayRedact`. The page checked every file's BLAKE3 and the version itself before pinning. Help and about in the page name the version and commit. The earlier in-tab mode, with sample people as engines in the tab on version `082f1b03`, is removed. A browser that ran it keeps that mode's IndexedDB databases (`uor-comms:holo-messenger-<sample person>`); each load deletes them if they are there, with their markers in localStorage. Its kept chats are met by a "from an earlier version" screen that offers Start fresh.

How the page loads it, as uor-comms `host/README.md` says: it fetches `manifest.json` and the eight files from `uor-foundation.github.io/uor-comms/engine/<version>/`, checks that the manifest names the pinned version and commit (the commit is the manifest's own claim: the version's BLAKE3 does not cover it), that each file's BLAKE3 is the manifest's, and that the version is the BLAKE3 of the manifest's file list. It imports the seven modules from blobs made of the checked bytes, with their relative imports pointed at the checked siblings' blobs. Only one import form is allowed: a whole line `import { names } from "./sibling.mjs";` naming a sibling already checked. If anything else in a module's text, comments and strings included, reads as an import or a re-export, the engine does not start; a dynamic import added to `trust.mjs` and re-pinned is refused. It loads `room-kernel.holo` with prism-code's `loadArchive` and requires it to verify. If anything fails, the engine does not start, nothing unchecked runs, and a screen says what failed and offers Try again. A changed `engine.mjs`, `trust.mjs`, `transport.mjs`, holo or manifest was each refused in the test. The engine files are fetched on every start.

What is real, said on the first start, in About and in the strip at the top: messages go between real browsers. Browsers find each other through ntfy.sh, a public server, on topics made from room and identity addresses, so anyone who has seen a room's address can see when its members connect. Connections use a public STUN server (Google's), and the people you connect to see your network address. There is no relay yet, so test on the same network, and both must be online at the same time. Nothing is encrypted yet; post-quantum encryption is planned. Keys travel in invite links until Prism ID. A message holds up to 4,096 bytes of text; a longer one is refused on the page, before the engine, with the count and a plain sentence.

Whose key is whose, as uor-comms `host/README.md` says ("Whose key is whose", decision 19): the page makes one 32-byte identity and one device key (`newDeviceKey`, the private half not exportable) in this browser, opens the device book (`deviceBook`) with them, and passes the book as signer and verdict to both `open` and `webrtcTransport`, with `ntfyRendezvous()`. Invite someone (Start a chat, Show my code, or Share on your own card) shows `book.invite(base)`, a `#uc1.` link and its picture code, and says that whoever has the link can reach you once and that it carries your device key. Opening such a link calls `transport.reach(book.accept(link))` and offers a chat with the inviter, with a field for the name you give them, through `findDirect` or `createDirect`; on a first visit the link waits until the identity is made and the first-run steps are done. The inviter's book learns the invitee's key from the one-time token when they connect. In a group, a member passes on the keys of the room's other members (decision 20, an introduction); the book records them like any other key, and the transport proves peers with the same book, so a person known from a shared group can be invited to another chat, and the group picker lists them. A person whose key the book does not hold cannot be reached or added, and the page says why. People show by the start of their identity: the engine has no names.

On the engine: the chat list, members and roles, creating a group and a direct chat, invite, join, decline and leave, remove, ban, lift a ban, change of role, sending (content version 1), the timeline in the host's order, and receiving. Which controls show is `offered(room, target, { granted })`, asked for 0, 50 and 100, so a role row shows only at a level the engine offers. A refusal shows one plain sentence with the engine's own word, for every reason the host names: `banned`, `closed`, `not joined`, `needs power`, `not allowed`, `unknown action`, `direct`, `unknown room`, `malformed`, `content`, `tampered`, `signature`, `device`, `a direct room names a target`, `unknown setting`, and the held `parents unknown`. `transport.onStatus` drives the strip, the chat list's engine row and a banner in a chat (offline, looking for people, connected). Sending, as uor-comms says (`host/README.md`, "Sending, and what is sent", and its ruling for this page): a clock while the engine signs and stores; one tick once it is announced, and nothing more until receipts exist. `transport.delivered` and `onDelivered` mean a proven peer holds the event or it was sent to one over an open channel, which does not confirm it was stored, so they drive only the outbox, never a tick: while an own message is in `engine.outbox()`, its info says it is waiting for a browser to take it and offers Try again, which calls `engine.retry(address)`. The page's `KNOWS.delivery` (the model's `engineKnowsDelivery`) stays false, so `Timeline.tickShown` never answers two ticks or the accent (`noDoubleTickWithoutDelivery`, `noAccentWithoutRead`). Before `send` is called the page turns CRLF and CR into LF and checks the engine's content limits, kept in one place (`ENGLIMIT`; `content.mjs` exports no numbers, so they are taken from uor-comms CONFORMANCE RK-16 and `Comms.ContentProofs`): text of 1 to 4,096 bytes of UTF-8 after NFC, no NUL, at most 50 mentions. Each refusal is one plain dialog and the text stays in the box. Reaction keys are not checked on the page: it sends only the quick reactions and picked emoji, and the engine checks the key.

Removal: when someone removes or bans you, the event reaches your browser; the chat stays in your list, readable, the composer replaced by who removed or banned you and what you can do, and a toast says so once. It stays so after a reload. Under the roles help one line says that a message a banned person wrote before their ban reached a browser may still show there (uor-comms: soft-fail is not modelled).

Paging, as uor-comms `host/README.md` says ("Asking and receiving", `timeline`): the page reads a chat in windows. A chat opens on the engine's newest window (50 events); scrolling up asks for the next 50 with the window's first event as `before`, through the page's scroll-up path for long chats, and the row in view keeps its place (rows stay keyed, and the row is found again by its message when grouping changes its key). While earlier events exist the top of the chat says "Earlier messages load as you scroll up"; the room's intro shows only once `hasMore` is false. Each read of the engine asks again for the window from the oldest event shown to the newest, paging back with `before` until that event is in it, so an event that arrives late inside what is shown takes its place without moving the reader. The page reads the whole room (`eventsOf`) only where a rule needs it: whether a room is direct (its creating event, read once), who invited you, and the last change that concerns you (removed or banned). The named reasons `limit`, `unknown event` and `unknown room` are in the refusal table. What needed the whole history, and what each does now: the chat-list preview and its time are the newest event, always in the newest window; the unread count and the mention mark count the loaded window, and the badge shows a plus ("50+", read out as "or more") when every loaded message is unread and earlier ones exist, because unread is not kept across reloads and paging back for it would load every chat whole; jump to the first unread goes to the first unread one loaded; search, in this chat or all chats, first reads each engine chat back to its start, at most 5,000 events, and says which chats it stopped short in; the media, links and files card says it covers the messages loaded so far.

Message text, read in place: a message's content is fetched when its event is admitted, not when it is drawn. Drawing reads the text from this browser's store only (`browserStore.get`), never waiting on the network. A small queue in the page asks `engine.content(address)` for every admitted message whose text the store does not hold (`store.has`): three at a time, the newest first; what no connected browser holds is asked again whenever the set of connected browsers changes. After `restore()`, once the chats show, a catch-up queues every kept message whose text is missing, so a browser that already has the gap heals once a holder is online, with no scrolling; only the rows that showed "has not arrived in full yet" are read again, and the rest of the drawn rows stay. No size cap beyond the three at a time: message content version 1 holds at most 4,096 bytes of text and no attachment. Events after my removal from a room are not fetched (the engine sends none). Redaction is not wired in this engine version, so there is no redacted event to skip.

Edits, reactions and deleting, as uor-comms `host/README.md` says ("Asking and receiving": `messages`, `redact`). What moved from the page to the engine: the page no longer decodes message content itself, nor keeps edits and reactions of its own. Engine chats are drawn from `messages(room, { before, limit })`, asked for as many messages as the shown timeline window holds; `timeline` stays the spine for the order and for membership changes, and each message event takes its row from `messages()` by address. An edit is content kind 2 naming the message and a reaction is content kind 3 with its key, both sent with `engine.send(room, content)` (content.mjs; CONFORMANCE RK-16: an edit and a reaction name the event they concern); the engine applies them: an edit counts only from the message's sender and the last wins, a reaction once per member. Taking a reaction back redacts every address in `message.mine[key]` ("redacting them all takes the reaction back"; a list, since two of my devices may both react). Delete shows on an engine message when `engine.mayRedact(room, event)` says the redaction would be accepted, read for each message shown and again when an event lands in the room, as `offered()` is; the page no longer reads the redaction rule itself, and a refusal stays the fallback. Delete calls `engine.redact`; every browser then shows a neutral "Message deleted" from `timeline`'s `redacted`, never the text, and the page drops any text it had shown. The delete dialog says it is removed for everyone, that each browser in the chat deletes its copy of the text when the deletion reaches it (one that is offline does so when it comes back), and that nothing is encrypted yet. The redaction events themselves, edits and reactions have no row. A redacted event whose kind this browser never learned shows as a deleted message: the README says a redacted message and a redacted reaction look the same there. Since #29 deletes redacted content from every store, the page keeps the addresses of the edits and reactions it has seen (in its own database), so a reaction taken back does not show as a deleted message on this browser, also after a reload. Engine chats are drawn from what this browser's store holds (`messages()` never waits on the network); the content queue fetches what `messages()` cannot show yet, newest first, and the row is drawn once its text lands. The content queue does not fetch content of redacted events. Content still missing shows "has not arrived in full yet" as before.

Room settings, as uor-comms `host/README.md` says ("Asking and receiving": `state`, `changeSetting`, `mayChangeSetting`). An engine room's name, description and picture are read from `engine.state(room)`: each is a content address, and the page reads the bytes from this browser's store, else asks `engine.content(address)` once and draws them when they land (text as UTF-8, a picture as the room picture); what no connected browser had is asked again when the connected browsers change, and until then the room shows as unnamed and its info says part of its name, description or picture has not arrived. The page keeps no room name, description or picture of its own any more; records an earlier version kept in its database are deleted on load. A room the page creates is named, described, pictured and, if chosen, opened with `changeSetting` right after `createRoom`. The edit dialog shows each field only where `mayChangeSetting` accepts a change of it (asked with null, since the rule reads the key and bytes passed would be stored), and saving calls `changeSetting` for each changed field; a refusal stays the fallback, in plain words. The roles card reads who can join and each action's power from `state`, and shows a choice (Invite only or Open; Everyone, Moderators or Admins) only where `mayChangeSetting` accepts it. Each change is a line in the chat on every browser, from the timeline: the name, description and picture with the model's sentences (the new name when this browser holds it), opening and powers with the neutral "changed the room". Who may remove, ban, change a role or send follows `offered()` as before; with the new defaults a power-0 member sees none of the first three, and a forced one says "Your role in this room is too low for this" with `needs power`.

Known limits of this engine version (`bed745b`, unchanged from `82dae7a`): a room's name, description and picture reach only the browser that set them, since a peer serves content only when an admitted message names it (`engine.carries`) and a change of setting is not a message, so the other members see the room unnamed; opening a room lets a person join without an invitation, but the transport passes a room only to the people it lists as invited or joined, so nobody else holds it to join; a redacted message's text is deleted from each browser's store when the redaction reaches it, but a copy taken out of the engine before that is beyond its reach; an event that cites the room as it was before a ban may still be admitted (soft-fail is not modelled); there is no relay and no encryption.

Kept in this browser: the engine's `browserStore`, IndexedDB `uor-comms:holo-messenger-device`; the page's IndexedDB `holo-messenger-engine`, holding the identity and device key, the book's saved state, whom to look for after a reload, the edits and reactions this browser has seen, your name and the names you give; `hm-engine-on`; and the page's preferences under `hm-local` and `hm-set`. The sample mode's storage (`hm-ui`, `hm-id`, `hm-lv`, and the `hm-set` entries only it used: name, avatar, receipts, typing, media, invitations) is deleted on load, and the Privacy panel says so; the engine's data is not touched. On a reload the page calls `restore()` once, which admits every kept event again, parents first. Measured in headless Chromium on 2026-11-21 with this pin, three reloads each: `restore()` 4 to 5 ms with no messages, 85 to 106 ms with 50, 1,012 to 1,066 ms with 500, the chats showing 123 to 498, 216 to 246 and 1,155 to 1,201 ms after the load began. If the store was cleared by the browser, the key is gone, or the kept chats are from the earlier in-tab engine mode, the page shows none of it, says what happened, and offers Start fresh. Sign out deletes everything: the engine databases, the key, the book, the names and the preferences.

Tests on 2026-11-21, headless Chromium on a local ntfy subset with the published engine unless named: `real.cjs` 270 (in place of the sample regression, at 320, 412 and 1280 in dark and light), `rules.cjs` 114 (the client rules on real data, in place of `rules14.cjs` and the rule part of the wa-ui drive), `start.cjs` 60 (the strip, About, a link and `#engine` on a first visit, the old storage deleted, tampered files refused, restore timings), `typing.cjs` 21, and the engine suites carried over: messages 27, paging 20, content 14, groups 45, room settings 46; engine unit checks 6 (newer content) and 34 of 36 (settings: the two misses are the delivery bug above); copies against the model 8,845 inputs, 0 differ; a two-browser run against the real ntfy.sh with the engine fetched from GitHub Pages, 10 of 10.

| 2 notify | enabled, room mode, mention, viewing, own | none, badge, notify, highlight |
| 3 message action | action, own, status, moderator, failed | offered, hidden |
| 4 link | kind, known, status | home, room, preview, profile, settings |
| 5 Enter | shift, modifier, modifier required, popup open | send, newline, complete |
| 6 link scheme | scheme | opens, blocked |
| 7 panes | width in pixels, two bytes | one, two, three |

| 12 day label | days ago | today, yesterday, weekday, date |

Operation 9 is not used. Anything else answers `invalid request`.

## Known limits

- Thresholds and budgets in the modules are the accepted Phase 1 values (2026-11-18). None is measured in a gate.
